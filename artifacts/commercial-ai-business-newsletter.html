Executive Thesis

Commercial AI is becoming a governed participant in real work

The strongest products combine AI with a domain constraint, a measurable decision, and an accountable human owner.

Shared Operating Pattern

Test, govern, and verify

The three products differ technically, but each places AI inside a controlled loop. Physics simulation constrains medical robotics, institution-approved knowledge constrains banking responses, and deterministic EDA software verifies engineering-agent results.

01 · Healthcare Robotics

NVIDIA Medical Physics Simulation

Moves early robot training and failure exploration into reusable, GPU-accelerated simulation before hardware-heavy testing.

Process
Parallel virtual training and testing
Decision
Which design should proceed to physical validation?
Control
Engineers must verify simulation-to-reality transfer.
02 · Financial Services

Glia Precision AI Response Modes

Lets banks tune generative freedom by topic instead of applying one risk setting to every customer interaction.

Process
Strict, Rephrase, or Compose by topic
Decision
How much AI discretion is appropriate here?
Control
The institution owns content, risk mapping, and review.
03 · Semiconductor Engineering

Siemens Fuse EDA AI Agent

Adds continuous validation against deterministic, physics-based engineering tools during long-running agent workflows.

Process
Agent orchestration with embedded validation
Decision
Is the result valid enough for engineer review?
Control
EDA checks support—not replace—design authority.

Research Integrity

During portfolio conversion, two cases in the original draft could not be corroborated in company newsrooms. Vendor-reported performance and adoption figures are clearly labeled rather than presented as independent proof.

Client Application

Five questions before approving an AI pilot

  1. ProcessWhich step is removed, shortened, or improved?
  2. EvidenceIs the performance claim independently measured or vendor-reported?
  3. BoundaryWhat can the system recommend, execute, or never do?
  4. OversightWho approves high-impact actions and handles exceptions?
  5. MetricWill success be measured through cost, time, quality, risk, revenue, or customer outcomes?
